# The Scarlet Wood — World & Setting Guide

---

## Core Premise of the World

The Scarlet Wood is a **living, inherently magical forest** inhabited by anthropomorphic animal peoples (forestkin) whose cultures, politics, and identities are inseparable from the land itself. Magic here is not an abstract force or spellcasting system—it is **ecological, ancestral, and embodied**. The Wood remembers. The Wood bleeds. The Wood can be stolen.

Beyond its borders lies the **manfolk world**: industrial, extractive, and fundamentally incompatible with the Wood’s mode of existence. Contact between these worlds is asymmetrical—**the forest is known, mapped, sampled, and exploited**, while the forestkin understand very little about the scale or logic of human civilization.

This is not a hidden world. It is an **endangered one**.

---

## Geography & Spatial Logic

### The Scarlet Wood
- Dense, ancient forest with scarlet-hued foliage and subtle ambient magic
- Magic manifests as:
  - Resonance in wood grain
  - Unnatural durability or responsiveness
  - Faint internal glow when harvested or repurposed
- Clear-cut areas feel *wrong*: quiet, cold, and spiritually hollow
- The deeper one travels inward, the less compatible human technology becomes

### The Borderlands
- Trails, gullies, and logging roads act as **wounds** in the land
- These liminal zones are where most early conflicts occur
- The forest pushes back subtly at first: overgrowth, lost paths, animal resistance

### Beyond the Wood
- Vast, industrial, and distant
- Represented through artifacts (paperwork, vehicles, devices) rather than direct travel
- Human infrastructure feels overwhelming in scale and momentum

## Factions of the Scarlet Wood

Each faction represents a **philosophy of survival**, not just a profession.

### The Deep-Root Kin
- Non-mammalian forestkin (snakes, spiders, amphibians, insects)
- Operate through secrecy, memory, and patience
- Value long timelines and unseen influence
- Often aware of threats long before others

### The Earth-Movers
- Large-bodied mammals (bears, beavers, etc.)
- Builders, fortifiers, and defenders
- Believe survival comes through preparation and physical resistance
- First to escalate when borders are breached

### The Moss-Fur Clan
- Small to medium mammals (mice, moles, hedgehogs)
- Farmers, caretakers, and cultural backbone
- Bear the greatest cost when violence escalates
- Often morally conflicted between safety and justice

### The Scrapsmiths
- Inventors, tinkerers, and adapters (raccoons, foxes)
- Bridge magic and material ingenuity
- Increasingly forced to respond to industrial threats with innovation
- Risk mirroring the enemy’s methods

### The Sky-Sailors
- Fliers and canopy-dwellers (hawks, owls, squirrels)
- Scouts, messengers, and early-warning system
- Understand scale, movement, and distance better than others
- First to recognize the *size* of WoodCo’s reach

## The Manfolk World

### Lumberjack Billy & Sons
- Small-time subcontractors
- Ignorant, greedy, and disposable
- Serve as the **point of first contact** and moral ambiguity
- Their destruction marks a permanent escalation

### WoodCo
- Multinational logging and resource-extraction corporation
- Bureaucratic, compartmentalized, and relentless
- Views the Scarlet Wood as:
  - A resource
  - A research opportunity
  - A strategic asset
- Operates through layers: contractors, agents, R&D, logistics

### WoodCo R&D
- Fully aware that the Scarlet Wood is *different*
- Uses clinical language: “resonance,” “samples,” “viability”
- Has begun integrating stolen wood into machines
- Treats magic as fuel, not life

---

## Technology & Magic

### Forest Magic
- Subtle, pervasive, ecological
- Stronger in untouched areas
- Weakens or warps when removed from the Wood

### Human Technology
- Loud, invasive, efficient
- Functions well at the borders
- Requires adaptation (or stolen magic) to penetrate deeper

### Magitech (WoodCo)
- A violation, not a synthesis
- Machines built *with* Scarlet Wood, not alongside it
- Visually unsettling: glowing interiors, organic shapes under steel

---

## Social & Political Reality

- The council is **not unified**—it is a pressure valve
- Youth are increasingly forced into adult decisions by external threat
- Violence has consequences beyond victory
- Martyrdom changes culture

The Scarlet Wood is transitioning from **a place worth defending** to **a place preparing for war**.

---

## Narrative Rules for the Setting

When generating content, NPC actions, or future events:

- The world should always feel **larger than the PCs**
- Victories invite retaliation, not resolution
- Magic has a cost when removed from its ecosystem
- Industrial forces escalate through logistics, not emotion
- The forest responds slowly—but remembers everything

---

## Campaign Trajectory Context

Sessions 1–7 establish:
- First contact
- Moral escalation
- Proof of external awareness
- The beginning of organized resistance

From this point forward, the world is in **open transition**.

There is no return to innocence.

Only adaptation, resistance, or loss.
