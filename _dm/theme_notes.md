# The Scarlet Wood
## Campaign Tone, Themes, and Guiding Principles

This document defines the **core feel, narrative intent, and thematic boundaries** of *The Scarlet Wood*. It is intended as a **guiding reference** for GMs, writers, and AI agents contributing content, ensuring tonal and thematic consistency across sessions, NPCs, encounters, and story arcs.

---

## 1. CORE FANTASY PILLAR

**Inherently-magical, anthropomorphic forestfolk defending their living homeland against industrial exploitation by an uncaring, rational, and well-resourced outside force.**

This is not a story about discovering heroism.
It is a story about **being forced into it**.

---

## 2. TONAL IDENTITY

### Primary Tone
- **Melancholic resistance**
- **Earnest, not ironic**
- **Grounded fantasy** rather than mythic high fantasy
- Violence is consequential, never cartoonish

### Emotional Palette
- Early: curiosity, youthful defiance, adventure
- Middle: anger, fear, grief, responsibility
- Late: resolve, sacrifice, transformation

Moments of warmth, humor, and camaraderie exist—but they are **fragile**, increasingly rare, and worth protecting.

---

## 3. THEMATIC AXES

### A. Nature vs. Industry (Without Simplification)
- The forest is **alive, old, and reactive**, but not benevolent
- Industry is **efficient, logical, profit-driven**, not mustache-twirling evil
- WoodCo does not hate the forest—it **objectifies** it

The horror lies in **instrumental thinking applied to a living world**.

---

### B. Escalation Has a Cost
- Every violent act changes the political and magical landscape
- Deniability disappears quickly
- Success invites stronger responses

There are **no clean victories**, only survivable ones.

---

### C. Youth Forced Into Leadership
- PCs begin as trainees, scouts, and helpers
- They become symbols before they are ready
- Adults and elders often hesitate, fracture, or fail

The tragedy is not that the PCs fight.
The tragedy is that **someone their age has to**.

---

### D. Magic as a Living System
- Scarlet magic is **resonant, reactive, and unstable**
- It responds to harm, fear, and intention
- It does not obey factions or councils

Magic is not a tool—it is **a relationship**.

WoodCo’s greatest mistake is treating it like fuel.

---

## 4. MORAL LANDSCAPE

### No Absolute Innocence
- Lumberjack Billy & Sons were culpable, but disposable
- WoodCo is structurally violent, not personally hateful
- The forestfolk are justified—but capable of cruelty

The campaign does not reward nihilism or purity.
It rewards **considered action under pressure**.

---

### Mercy vs. Survival
- Mercy may preserve the soul but endanger the forest
- Brutality may protect the Wood but fracture its people

The correct choice is rarely clear.

---

## 5. FACTION PHILOSOPHIES (DEFAULT STANCES)

These are tendencies, not absolutes.

- **Moss-Fur Clan**: Preservation of life, fear of escalation, communal survival
- **Deep-Root Kin**: Long memory, inevitability of conflict, quiet ruthlessness
- **Earth-Movers**: Defense through strength, preparation, and visible deterrence
- **Scrap-Smiths**: Adaptation, innovation, uncomfortable similarity to industry
- **Sky-Sailors**: Information, mobility, warning signs, emotional distance

Factions may disagree sharply while still loving the forest.

---

## 6. ANTAGONISTS: HOW THEY SHOULD FEEL

### Lumberjack Billy & Sons
- Human-scale, tragic, frightened
- Loud, clumsy, dangerous
- Tools of a larger system

They exist to show **what happens to small people under big systems**.

---

### WoodCo
- Patient
- Well-documented
- Bureaucratically violent

WoodCo does not rush.
WoodCo observes, records, analyzes, then optimizes.

Their greatest weapon is not machines—it is **process**.

---

### WoodCo R&D
- Curious, amoral, fascinated
- Views magic as a solvable problem
- Increasingly willing to take risks

They are not villains because they hate life.
They are villains because they **do not consider it**.

---

## 7. THE FOREST ITSELF

The Scarlet Wood is:
- A character
- A witness
- A participant

It remembers violence.
It reacts to theft.
It may protect—or punish.

As the campaign progresses, the forest should feel:
- Less passive
- Less predictable
- More dangerous

---

## 8. GUIDING RULES FOR FUTURE CONTENT

When creating new content, ask:

1. **Does this escalation feel earned?**
2. **Does this choice create new problems, not solve old ones?**
3. **Is the emotional cost visible?**
4. **Would this make sense if viewed through WoodCo’s lens?**
5. **Does the forest react, even subtly?**

If the answer is "yes" to most of these, the content belongs.

---

## 9. FINAL STATEMENT

*The Scarlet Wood* is not about winning.

It is about **what must be protected**,
**what must be sacrificed**,
and **what the defenders become in the process**.

The question at the heart of the campaign is simple:

> **How much of the forest can be saved without becoming something else entirely?**
